Sources

Where to go next, for the original text and the works it inspired.

This site is a guide and a starting point, not a substitute for the primary texts. “The Drover’s Wife” has been in the public domain for many years, and copies of the original story are easy to find through any library or reputable literary archive. The works listed below are a good next stop.

Primary text

Henry Lawson, “The Drover’s Wife”

First published in The Bulletin, 1892; collected in While the Billy Boils, Angus & Robertson, 1896. The original story this whole site is built around.

Collection

While the Billy Boils

Lawson’s first and best-known short story collection, gathering his Bulletin-era bush sketches, including “The Drover’s Wife.” The single best place to read his other work in the same vein.

Painting

Russell Drysdale, The Drover’s Wife (1945)

Held in the collection of the National Gallery of Australia, Canberra. Worth seeking out in reproduction alongside the story it responds to.

Short story

Murray Bail, “The Drover’s Wife”

A metafictional response built around the Drysdale painting rather than the Lawson text. Anthologised together with Lawson’s and Barbara Jefferis’s versions in various Australian short story collections exploring the same character.

Play / novel / film

Leah Purcell, The Drover’s Wife: The Legend of Molly Johnson

Purcell’s reimagining exists in three forms worth experiencing separately: the original stage play (Belvoir St Theatre, 2016), the novel (2019), and the feature film she wrote, directed, and starred in (2021).

Biography

Henry Lawson (1867–1922)

For biographical detail beyond this site, any major Australian literary reference work or library catalogue will have a full entry on Lawson’s life, letters, and complete published works.

A note on this site

An independent guide

This site was built as an independent reader’s guide to Lawson’s story and is not affiliated with his estate, his publishers, or any of the later artists discussed here. Direct quotations from “The Drover’s Wife” are kept brief and clearly marked; for the full text, please seek out the original story.
